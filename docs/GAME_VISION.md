# Game Vision

## Working identity

A darkly comic, high-tension, non-combat social-deduction roguelite built around ordinary roles in closed environments, private agendas, incomplete information, procedural truth generation, and escalating push-your-luck decisions.

The player should repeatedly experience the feeling: **"I know something is wrong, but I do not yet know what — or whether I am part of it."**

## Player fantasy

The player is not a superhero. They are someone who belongs in the location: a clerk, guest, attendant, juror, guard, prisoner, waiter, conductor, investigator, or other plausible participant.

Their public Role creates obligations and an expected routine. Their private Agenda gives them a reason to bend, exploit, or hide within that routine. The player's skill comes from reading people, managing perception, assembling incomplete evidence, using social capital, and deciding when certainty is good enough.

## Core pillars

### 1. Ordinary behavior hides extraordinary motives
Players and NPCs should usually do plausible things. Suspicion emerges from context, timing, contradiction, access, and motive rather than obvious "crime actions."

### 2. Lies are not proof of guilt
NPCs may lie to protect affairs, theft, debts, embarrassment, careers, relationships, unrelated schemes, or other private objectives. A central mystery must coexist with unrelated secrets.

### 3. Self-knowledge can be incomplete
Inspired by paranoia mechanisms such as Scape Goat, the player can know their own actions while still being uncertain about their significance. A briefing may be wrong, a supposed ally may be false, the player may be framed, infected, manipulated, or unknowingly carrying something important.

### 4. The run produces a build
The persistent build is the player's selected Approach and equipped Edges. The run build emerges from temporary Assets, relationships, access, information, and synergies. A run should create a recognizable playstyle without requiring combat.

### 5. Push your luck is a recurring decision
The player repeatedly chooses between resolving with imperfect information and continuing to investigate, manipulate, hide, or complete objectives while time and Exposure worsen.

### 6. Progression expands the possibility space
Persistent progression should unlock new Edges, Assets, Roles, Agendas, incidents, Stakes, locations, and campaign discoveries rather than simply increasing numerical power.

## Tone

**Darkly humorous and somewhat cruel, with the ability to become genuinely tense.**

The world should support absurd social situations, awkward lies, bad alibis, petty motives, and comic escalation. The same visual language must also support an empty corridor, a suspicious absence, an impossible timeline, or the realization that the player is being framed.

Target tonal rule: **funny until suddenly it is not.**

## Presentation direction

### Camera
Current preferred direction: **elevated third-person 3/4 perspective**.

The camera should be close enough to preserve individual characters and expressive animation, but wide enough to read nearby movement, doors, watchers, routes, and environmental context. It should not be a distant Diablo-style isometric camera and should not default to first-person.

Conversation/interrogation moments may temporarily tighten framing.

### Visual style
Current preferred direction: **graphic-noir cel shading**.

Principles:
- stylized 3D characters and environments;
- strong silhouettes and ink-like outlines;
- angular, expressive forms;
- restrained environmental palette with selective information colors;
- hand-drawn or hatched shadow language;
- comic-panel framing or graphic typography for major reveals;
- readable rather than photorealistic animation.

The goal is not to imitate any existing game's copyrighted art, but to combine the readability and exaggeration of cel-shaded games with a darker illustrated-noir sensibility.

## Session targets

### Full-game run target
Approximately **25–35 minutes**, varying by location.

Suggested pacing:
- Arrival / Routine: 4–6 min
- Complication: 6–8 min
- Incident: 7–10 min
- Escalation: 5–7 min
- Resolution: 3–5 min

### Prototype target
The first complete Hotel run should be **8–12 minutes**. If that compact loop is not compelling, additional locations and progression will not fix it.

## Initial locations

- Hotel — access and routines
- Bullet train — stations and departures
- Estate — relationships and social history
- Airplane — confinement and manifests
- Prison — routine, alliances, institutional access
- Courthouse / Jury — information restriction and deliberation

Each location must alter rules and pressure, not merely provide a different skin.

## Campaign premise under evaluation

Apparently unrelated incidents contain repeating placements: changed rooms, altered tickets, unexpected invitations, seat changes, transfers, jury selection anomalies, and other coincidences that place specific people together at specific times.

The player gradually discovers that **someone or something is arranging encounters**.

The final explanation is intentionally not locked. Grounded conspiracy, psychological/horror, and speculative/weird campaign truths will be developed and compared before production commitment.

## Current working-title direction

No title is locked.

Current useful references include:
- Loose Ends
- Bad Alibis
- Ulterior
- Mixed Motives
- False Pretenses
- The Arrangement

Final naming should follow the settled campaign truth and tone rather than lead them.
