# Campaign Truth Options

## Campaign question

Across unrelated incidents, the player discovers repeating anomalies of **placement**:
- hotel room changes;
- last-minute train tickets;
- unexplained invitations;
- airline seat changes;
- prison transfers;
- jury-selection anomalies;
- people repeatedly arriving in places they statistically or administratively should not have been.

The central long-term question is:

> **Who or what is arranging these encounters, and what outcome are they trying to create?**

The final answer is intentionally not locked. It must be worth discovering after dozens of runs and should recontextualize earlier play.

---

# Option A — The Outcome Market

## Genre
Grounded conspiracy / intelligence / dark satire.

## Truth
A hidden organization learned that large outcomes can be manipulated more reliably by arranging **small social collisions** than by directly controlling events.

They do not order someone to commit a crime. They change a hotel room. Upgrade a ticket. Delay a train. Move a prisoner. Place a juror. Invite one unexpected guest.

The resulting arguments, friendships, mistakes, affairs, discoveries, crimes, rescues, and coincidences create the desired outcome while preserving deniability.

Over time this capability became a market. Governments, corporations, criminals, wealthy clients, and internal factions purchase **conditions**, not actions.

Examples:
- ensure two people meet;
- prevent a witness from reaching someone;
- make a scandal statistically likely;
- create a believable scapegoat;
- encourage a deal without either party knowing it was engineered;
- destabilize a jury without bribing a juror directly.

## Why it fits gameplay
Every existing system becomes thematic:
- Roles are cover/normal life;
- Agendas intersect unexpectedly;
- Leverage is how individuals bend local outcomes;
- the campaign reveals a larger entity doing the same thing at scale;
- Stakes resemble increasingly complex engineered conditions;
- the player eventually realizes they have been learning the same craft as the organization.

## Potential final twist
The player is not merely investigating the system. Their progression data has been used to evaluate them as a prospective **arranger**.

Endgame choices could include exposure, destruction, takeover, infiltration, or using the system to reverse previous harm.

## Risks
- could become a generic secret-cabal story if characters/factions are weak;
- needs a convincing reason the player can see many unrelated incidents;
- requires restraint to avoid implausibly omnipotent conspirators.

# Option B — The Witness Problem

## Genre
Psychological horror / unreliable identity / paranoia.

## Truth
The incidents are real, but the player's relationship to them is not what the campaign interface implies.

The player is repeatedly reconstructing events from fragmented testimony, records, simulations, and incomplete memories because one catastrophic unresolved event depended on understanding **how ordinary people misread each other**.

The repeating placements are artifacts of a hidden selection process: these particular incidents share people, behavioral patterns, or causal structures linked to that central event.

As the campaign advances, the player discovers that the system classifying people as murderer, witness, scapegoat, accomplice, innocent, etc. has itself been making dangerous assumptions.

## Why it fits gameplay
- source provenance and certainty become central to the narrative;
- self-uncertainty becomes psychologically meaningful;
- replayed procedural incidents can represent comparative reconstruction rather than literal repeated history;
- the player learns that labels/Agendas are models, not necessarily objective morality.

## Potential final twist
The player's own identity/role in the originating event has been classified differently by different surviving sources. There may be no single clean label for what they were.

The final campaign asks the player to make a consequential judgment using the same imperfect evidence principles they have practiced throughout the game.

## Risks
- memory/reconstruction twists are common and can feel cheap;
- could undermine the player's sense that run events matter if framed as "just simulations";
- harder to reconcile with comedic tone and broad future content;
- must never erase deliberate player actions via arbitrary amnesia.

# Option C — The Convergence Engine

## Genre
Speculative thriller / probability horror / dark absurdism.

## Truth
A research program discovered that certain people behave like **causal catalysts**: when specific combinations of individuals share a place and time, unlikely chains of events become dramatically more probable.

The effect may be mathematical, technological, psychological, or something not fully understood. The important fact is that the organization can predict **convergences** but cannot perfectly predict what they produce.

So it arranges meetings:
- same hotel;
- same train;
- adjacent airplane seats;
- same estate weekend;
- prison transfer;
- jury pool.

Sometimes the result is a murder. Sometimes an invention, confession, romance, disaster, exposure of corruption, political shift, or bizarre accident.

The organization is not necessarily trying to cause crimes. It is running controlled bets on human possibility.

## Why it fits gameplay
- procedural variability becomes part of the fiction;
- the same people can recur naturally;
- Stakes can eventually represent increasingly volatile convergence conditions;
- unusual coincidences can escalate without requiring supernatural monsters/combat;
- dark humor fits researchers/clients attempting to quantify deeply human chaos.

## Potential final twist
The player is one of the strongest catalysts. Their presence is why incidents around them keep becoming unusually consequential.

The campaign may culminate in an intentionally engineered convergence involving characters from multiple locations, where the player must decide whether to cooperate with, sabotage, or redirect the experiment.

## Risks
- can drift into hand-wavey pseudoscience if rules are not coherent;
- might make human motives feel secondary to a cosmic/probability mechanism;
- requires careful tone to remain grounded enough for deduction.

# Hybrid possibility — The Market discovered Convergence

Option A and C can combine:

A grounded organization discovered a real but poorly understood convergence phenomenon and **commercialized it**.

Early campaign feels like conspiracy. Midgame reveals prediction/placement technology. Late game reveals that the organization is less in control than it claims.

This offers:
- grounded early mystery;
- dark corporate satire;
- escalating horror/weirdness;
- a reason arrangements sometimes create unintended disasters;
- factions that disagree over whether the system should be controlled, sold, studied, or destroyed.

This hybrid is currently a strong candidate but is not accepted canon.

---

# Evaluation criteria before locking canon

Score each option against:
1. payoff after 30–50+ hours;
2. ability to foreshadow fairly;
3. whether the reveal recontextualizes earlier runs;
4. fit with dark humor;
5. fit with high tension/horror;
6. compatibility with all planned locations;
7. support for recurring characters;
8. support for future locations/DLC/sequels;
9. whether player choices can meaningfully affect the ending;
10. whether the explanation enhances rather than diminishes procedural generation;
11. whether the player can understand the core truth without reading lore dumps;
12. whether the final act can provide a mechanically distinctive climax without conventional combat.

Do not lock the final title until this decision is substantially settled.
